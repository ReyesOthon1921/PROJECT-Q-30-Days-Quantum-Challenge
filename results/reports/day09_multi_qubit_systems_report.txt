Day 09 Lab: Multi-Qubit Systems
PROJECT-Q 30-Day Quantum Computing Challenge
============================================================

Main ideas:
1. Multiple qubits form one combined quantum system.
2. Tensor products combine state spaces by multiplication.
3. Two qubits have four computational basis states.
4. Three qubits have eight computational basis states.
5. In general, n qubits have 2^n basis states.

Basis state growth:
1 qubit(s): 2 basis states
2 qubit(s): 4 basis states
3 qubit(s): 8 basis states
4 qubit(s): 16 basis states
5 qubit(s): 32 basis states
6 qubit(s): 64 basis states
7 qubit(s): 128 basis states

Worked example counts:
{'11': 485, '01': 515}

Three-qubit superposition counts:
{'110': 114, '100': 109, '101': 141, '111': 130, '010': 126, '011': 125, '001': 141, '000': 114}

Lab conclusion:
Multi-qubit systems unlock larger quantum state spaces and prepare us for controlled gates, entanglement, and quantum algorithms.
